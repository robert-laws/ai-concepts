Understanding Tokenization

What are tokens?

Tokens are the basic units that LLMs use to understand text. They can be:

  • Whole words (like "hello")
  • Parts of words (like "ing" in "running")
  • Individual characters or punctuation
  • Spaces and special characters

As a rough estimate, 1 token ≈ 4 characters in English, or about 3/4 of a word.

Why Tokenization?

Breaking text into tokens allows the model to:

  • Handle any word, even ones it hasn't seen before
  • Work efficiently with mathematical operations
  • Understand patterns and relationships in language

Key Concepts to Remember

🔤 Tokenization

Text is split into smaller pieces (tokens) that the model can process mathematically.

📊 Embeddings

Each token is converted into a series of numbers (vectors) that represent its meaning.

🧠 Context

The model analyzes all tokens together to understand the full context and meaning.

✨ Generation

New tokens are predicted one at a time based on patterns learned from training data.

How Does This Actually Work?

  1. Input Text: You type a message or question
  2. Tokenization: The text is broken into tokens using a pre-defined vocabulary (typically 50,000-100,000+ tokens)
  3. Embedding: Each token is converted into a high-dimensional vector (series of numbers)
  4. Processing: These vectors flow through transformer layers that analyze relationships and context
  5. Prediction: The model predicts what token should come next
  6. Decoding: Predicted tokens are converted back into readable text
  7. Output: The generated text is returned to you
